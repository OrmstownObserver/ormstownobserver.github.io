Council wrap-up: Nov. 2, 2026

A new shop’s sign tied to the language Charter; a 15% contingency added to the 3e Rang culvert contract; new contract rules adopted.

The Ormstown Observer · Published November 4, 2026 · 6:30 p.m., hôtel de ville, 5 rue Gale · Watch the full recording on YouTube →
Observer Notes · Unofficial summary — official minutes pending

At a glance

  • A new shop’s sign at 55 rue Lambton was approved on condition that its English-only lettering be revised to comply with the Charter of the French language, and that its logos be harmonized.
  • A 15% contingency was added to the 3e Rang contract, given from the chair as $235,259.97 before tax, for a new maximum read as $1,803,659.74.
  • Two by-laws adopted: contract management (129-2026) and an amendment to the subdivision by-law (149.1-2026) exempting non-profits, schools and daycares from the parks contribution.
  • Three items were added at the opening and do not appear on the published agenda: a $25,000 budget transfer, a departure agreement with an employee, and the holiday office closure.
  • No councillor was heard voting against any item. The official minutes will confirm each result.
  • Official minutes are not published. This recap is built from the recording of the public session and the published agenda. Figures read aloud are given as stated at the sitting and are unconfirmed until the minutes appear.

Mayor’s remarks

The mayor said a traffic study ran from October 21 to 29 at four intersections, a first step, with the transport ministry, toward making them safer. Watch ▶︎

Repaving of the 3e Rang may slip, he said, because provincial bridge work has already closed one end of the road; doing the municipal work now would close it at both ends. He could not yet say whether the work will happen this year or next. Watch ▶︎

A meeting will bring together Super C’s neighbours, its managers and the municipality about odours. Watch ▶︎

The mayor also said the expense lists annexed to council minutes are raw data that mix operating costs with projects funded by grants and loans, such as the road work this year, which he put at $3.7 million, so adding them up does not show whether the budget is exceeded. He said the administration aims to publish budget updates in 2027. Watch ▶︎

First question period

Speaking for the Barrie Memorial Hospital ladies’ auxiliary, a resident announced its bazaar on Saturday, November 7, and said the auxiliary and the hospital foundation have pledged $1 million toward new imaging equipment. Watch ▶︎

The Observer’s editor asked whether the municipality had addressed the gap between when agendas are posted and the deadline for submitting written questions. The mayor said the matter is under review, that a new clerk arrived a few weeks ago, and that council is open to revisiting the deadline. Watch ▶︎

Agenda and minutes

Agenda2 — Adoption of the agenda, with three additions. The chair added items 4.6 (a transfer between budget families), 4.7 (a departure agreement with an employee) and 4.8 (the holiday closure of municipal offices). Watch ▶︎

Agenda3.1 — Minutes of the October 6, 2026 regular sitting.

Administration, Finance & Personnel

Agenda4.1 — Accounts payable. Council approved financial operations in a total given at the sitting as $174,687.29. The mayor said this list holds expenses only, while the annex attached to the minutes also includes salaries. Watch ▶︎

Agenda4.2 — Archives contract increased. Resolution 26-03-053 was amended to raise the cap on the Archives Lanaudière records-classification contract by $12,541.17, from $34,321.90 to $46,863.07, as read at the sitting, for additional work. Watch ▶︎

Agenda4.3 — Two comparative financial statements: tabled. The mayor said the Municipal Code requires them each year: one compares the year to date with the same period last year, the other projects the full year. Watch ▶︎

Agenda4.4 — Snow dumps shared with Franklin. The agreement letting Franklin use Ormstown’s two snow-dump sites was renewed, capped at 15 loads a season at $250 a load, as read at the sitting. Watch ▶︎

Agenda4.5 — Livestock Breeders Association as a potential emergency site. Its premises were recognized as a potential emergency site, at no cost to the municipality, effective once the association accepts the conditions in writing. Watch ▶︎

Added item 4.6 — Budget transfer. $25,000, as read at the sitting, moves from the budget family covering water testing and snow-dump maintenance to public-road maintenance. The mayor described it as year-end rebalancing between budget families. Watch ▶︎

Added item 4.7 — Departure agreement. Council authorized a negotiated departure agreement with an employee identified only by number. The mayor said the terms are confidential. Watch ▶︎

Added item 4.8 — Holiday closure. As read at the sitting, municipal offices close at noon on Wednesday, December 23 and reopen at 8:30 a.m. on Monday, January 4. Essential services remain available. Watch ▶︎

By-laws

Agenda5.1 — By-law 129-2026 on contract management: adopted. It replaces the previous contract-management by-law. The mayor said it applies to every municipal contract, bars splitting contracts to get around award rules, and includes measures against bid-rigging, collusion, conflicts of interest, intimidation and influence peddling. As he summarized it, contracts under $25,000 can be awarded by agreement, contracts of $25,000 or more but below the public-tender threshold go to written invitation, and larger ones go to public tender. Watch ▶︎ Our explainer on the draft →

Agenda5.2 — By-law 149.1-2026 amending the subdivision by-law: adopted. When land is subdivided, the owner must provide park land or pay 10% of its value. The amendment exempts registered non-profits active for the past two years, transfers of property for public purposes, schools, vocational and adult-education centres, and daycares and early-childhood centres. The mayor said two residents attended the August 26 public consultation. Watch ▶︎

Agenda5.3 — By-law 175-2026 repealing outdated by-laws: notice of motion, draft tabled. The mayor said the new clerk had identified the by-laws to repeal. Watch ▶︎

Municipal council

Agenda6.1 — Councillors’ updated declarations of pecuniary interests: tabled. Watch ▶︎

Public Works & Technical Services

Agenda7.1 — Aerated ponds: resolution 26-10-275 amended. The October decision to re-tender the emptying of the four aerated ponds now states that the money will be provided in the 2027 budget, a sentence the mayor said had been left out. Watch ▶︎

Agenda7.2 — 3e Rang contract ORM2025-08.1: 15% contingency added. The contract covers culvert work on the 3e Rang and chemin de la Rivière-Châteauguay. The mayor said a 15% allowance for unforeseen costs is standard but had been missing from the bid form. He read the contingency as $235,259.97 before tax and the contract’s maximum as $1,803,659.74. The contract was awarded on August 3 for $1,568,399.77 plus taxes (resolution 26-08-225); the contingency is 15% of that price, and the maximum read at the sitting equals the price plus the contingency. The mayor said the work is financed through a borrowing by-law. Watch ▶︎

Fire Safety

Agenda8.1 — Fire service report, September 2026: tabled. The chair gave 15 calls for the month. Watch ▶︎

Urban Planning

Agenda9.1 — Permits issued, October 2026: tabled. Six permits, for work valued at $1,623,635, as read at the sitting. Watch ▶︎

Agenda9.2 — PIIA, 55 rue Lambton: approved with conditions. Council accepted the proposed colours but required the English-only lettering on the façade sign to be revised to comply with the Charter of the French language, and the logos to be harmonized. Asked in the second question period whether the business name was the problem, the mayor said signage cannot be in English only and must include French, that inspectors had spoken with the owners, and that the municipality checks compliance with language law before granting a permit. Watch ▶︎

Observer note. The Observer has published an editorial on this decision, on what a PIIA review covers and who enforces the Charter: Editorial: A sign, the Charter, and who gets to decide.

Agenda9.3 — PIIA for a sign, 8 rue Church: approved as filed. An optician’s window film covering more of the windows than the 30% the zoning by-law allows, given at the sitting as about 50% of the front windows and 70.1% of the south side, was approved. The planning advisory committee cited the busy road and little foot traffic. Watch ▶︎

Culture & Recreation

Agenda10.1 — Library activity report. Listed on the agenda. At items 11 and 12, the chair said there was no correspondence and no other business. Watch ▶︎

Second question period

The Observer’s editor asked about the sign at 55 rue Lambton and whether the business name was the problem. The mayor’s answer is reported under item 9.2 above. Watch ▶︎
