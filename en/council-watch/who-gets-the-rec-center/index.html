Who gets the Rec-Center? Ormstown’s new policy for local non-profit organizations

Council has adopted a corrected version of its policy on recognizing community organizations — four months after adopting the first one — and the correction caps how much weekly room time any single group can claim.

The Ormstown Observer · Council Watch · October 15, 2026 · Regular sitting of September 8, 2026, item 10.5 · Watch the full recording on YouTube →

Ormstown council adopted a corrected version of its Politique de reconnaissance et de soutien aux organismes at the 8 September sitting, as item 10.5. Introducing it, the chair said the policy had first been adopted at the 4 May 2026 sitting, and that putting it into practice had revealed inconsistencies that needed correcting so it would be applied uniformly (01:24:49, 01:25:36). The published policy carries resolution number 26-09-255 and states that it replaces resolution 26-05-145 — a May 2026 number, consistent with that account. It was signed by the mayor and the director general, took effect the day it was adopted, and is now online.

Council also mandated the culture and leisure department to administer it.

For every club, league, committee and non-profit operating in Ormstown, this is now the document that sets out what the municipality offers them — and what it does not.

Five categories, and one that never applies

The policy recognizes five categories of organization: community and social; cultural and heritage; sports and leisure; environmental; and school or health establishments. That last category — government and para-government bodies such as schools, CLSCs, Élections Québec or Héma-Québec — does not have to apply at all. The policy states they are automatically considered recognized by the nature of their activities.

Everyone else applies. To be recognized, a group must be registered with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec as a non-profit (barring a justified exception), hold an annual general meeting, carry appropriate insurance, and show that its activities benefit residents of Ormstown and the Haut-Saint-Laurent. It must file its letters patent or constituting documents, an up-to-date list of board members, financial statements or an annual financial report, and an annual activity report. The application form and supporting documents go to the Service de la culture et des loisirs; the administration analyses the file and council decides by resolution.

What recognition actually gets you

Recognition may give access — subject to available resources and council decisions — to municipal rooms and facilities at a preferential rate, to certain municipal equipment, to promotion through municipal communication channels, to logistical support under the Programme de soutien logistique aux activités et aux événements, and to one-off or recurring financial aid under the Politique en matière d’aide financière, de partenariat et de bourse. The policy then says so plainly: recognition does not guarantee financial aid or logistical support.

The half-day rule

The substantive change is section 7.1. Each recognized organization gets priority booking at the preferential rate for a maximum of one half-day per week, per facility. A half-day means up to four consecutive hours, or another block the municipality sets — a morning, an afternoon, an evening.

Anything beyond that is an “additional slot.” Additional slots can be granted when space allows, but the policy calls them conditional, not guaranteed, and subject to review. If a recognized group that has not yet used its weekly priority half-day asks for a slot already held by another group on an additional basis, the municipality can reopen the allocation. It may then reassign the slot to the group that has not had its half-day, set up a rotation between organizations, offer an alternative time, or take any other measure aimed at sharing facilities fairly. Additional slots are never an acquired right.

The policy also reserves the municipality’s right to modify, suspend or cancel any slot — additional or not — without stating its reasons, with advance notice to the organization concerned where possible.

What you can do

Any non-profit operating in Ormstown can apply for recognized status. Request the application form from the Service de la culture et des loisirs, assemble the documents listed in section 5, and expect the decision to come as a council resolution. Read section 4 to find your category, section 7 to see what support attaches to it, and section 7.1 before you plan a weekly schedule — if your group currently holds a recurring slot beyond one half-day a week, that time is classified as additional and can be reviewed. Recognition can also be suspended or withdrawn by council resolution, but the policy guarantees the organization an opportunity to make its case first.
