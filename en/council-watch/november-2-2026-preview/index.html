What to watch

Fifteen sections. Two by-laws are listed for adoption, a roadwork contract is amended, and two earlier decisions come back to be modified. The agenda names each subject. It does not say what council will decide.

Items 5.1 and 5.2 — two by-laws up for adoption

AgendaItem 5.1 is the adoption of by-law 129-2026 on contract management, « abrogeant le Règlement 129-2021 ». Item 5.2 is the adoption of subdivision by-law 149.1-2026.

Official documentAgendaBy-law 129-2026. The draft was the subject of a notice of motion and was tabled at the October 6, 2026 sitting (item 5.1), where copies were made available to the public. It sets the rules the municipality follows to award contracts. Among other changes, it would allow direct awards (de gré à gré) only for contracts under $25,000; require a written invitation to at least two businesses from $25,000 up to the public-tender threshold; and lower from $50,000 to $25,000 the contract changes the director general may approve without council. The draft states that it replaces both by-law 129-2020 and by-law 129-2021. The November 2 agenda names only 129-2021. The Observer’s explainer sets out the draft in full.

Official documentUnder article 445 of the Code municipal, before a by-law is adopted, the clerk-treasurer or a council member must state its purpose and any changes made since the draft was tabled.

Official documentBy-law 149.1-2026. The first draft would add seven exemptions, paragraphs k) to q) of article 4.2 of subdivision by-law 149-2023, to the contribution for parks that applies to cadastral operations: non-profits offering free services to Ormstown residents and registered for two years; transfers for public purposes; operations by the municipality; a transitional lot to be transferred to an adjoining property; operations resulting from a judgment or a boundary determination (bornage); schools and adult or vocational training centres; and licensed daycares and CPEs. Its preamble gives July 6, 2026 as the date of the notice of motion. A public notice of consultation on 149.1-2026 has been posted on ormstown.ca.

MinutesCouncil adopted the first draft on August 3, 2026 (resolution 26-08-221).

Item 7.2 — a 15% contingency on the 3e Rang contract

AgendaItem 7.2 amends contract ORM2025-08.1, for works on the 3e Rang and chemin de la Rivière-Châteauguay, by adding a 15% contingency. The agenda gives no dollar amount and does not say what the percentage is calculated on.

MinutesCouncil awarded contract ORM2025-08.1 on August 3, 2026 (resolution 26-08-225) to Agence Chenail, the lowest conforming bid of the four received, at $1,568,399.77 before tax.

RecordingOn October 6, council authorized the engineering department to ask the transport ministry to move the 3e Rang work past the current grant deadline of December 31, 2026, because the ministry is replacing a bridge on the same road. The mayor said the request was meant to keep the provincial grant from being lost. The agenda does not say whether the contingency is connected to that request. Watch ▶︎

Item 7.1 — the aerated ponds, again

AgendaItem 7.1 modifies resolution 26-10-275, whose title the agenda quotes: authorization to return to tender in 2027 for the complete emptying of the four aerated ponds, and the transport and disposal of the sludge. The agenda does not say what is being modified.

RecordingAgendaThe title matches item 7.2 of the October 6 sitting. The mayor said then that the first call for tenders drew a single bid, which was above the budget. Council did not proceed with it and authorized a new call, with the money to be provided in the 2027 budget. Watch ▶︎

Item 4.3 — the comparative statements

Official documentArticle 176.4 of the Code municipal requires the clerk-treasurer (greffier-trésorier) to table two comparative statements each year. The first compares revenues and expenses so far this year with the same period last year. The second compares projected revenues and expenses for the full year with the budget. In a year without a general election, they are tabled at the last regular sitting held at least four weeks before the sitting where the budget is adopted.

AgendaItem 4.3 lists the two statements for 2025-2026. They show how the 2026 budget is tracking before council adopts the 2027 budget.

Item 6.1 — members’ interests

Official documentEach year, every council member must table an updated statement of pecuniary interests before council, no earlier than the second-to-last regular sitting and no later than the last (Loi sur les élections et les référendums dans les municipalités, art. 358, as amended in 2025). November 2 is the second-to-last regular sitting of 2026; the last is set for December 7.

AgendaItem 6.1 lists the tabling of those updates. The agenda does not say how many members’ statements are being tabled.

How to take part

The sitting is public and begins at 6:30 p.m. at 5 rue Gale. There are two question periods of thirty minutes each: a general one at the opening of the sitting (item 1.2), and a second near the close (item 14). Under by-law 2-2026 on the internal management of council, written questions must reach the clerk’s office at greffe@ormstown.ca by noon on the Friday before the sitting. For this sitting, that deadline was noon on Friday, October 30, and has passed. Questions can still be asked in person at either question period. Sittings are livestreamed and archived on the municipality’s YouTube channel.
