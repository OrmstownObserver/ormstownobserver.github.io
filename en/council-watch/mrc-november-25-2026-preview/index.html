What to watch

This is the sitting where the MRC adopts its 2027 budget and sets what each of its 13 municipalities will pay for it. The agenda also lists a new transit by-law with a 2027 bus fare schedule, a decision to renounce provincial aid for heritage restoration, the region’s position on Hydro-Québec’s wind call for tenders, and the next steps in rewriting the regional land-use plan. The agenda names each subject. It does not say what the mayors will decide.

The MRC’s regional council is made up of the mayors of its 13 municipalities, Ormstown’s included. Its decisions shape what each town pays to the MRC and the planning rules each town’s own by-laws must follow.

Item 6.2 — the 2027 budget, and what each town pays

AgendaThe budget is adopted part by part: mandatory powers, optional powers, the urban planning service, the pumping station, fire prevention, the bus service, adapted transport and building management. By-law 362-2026 (item 6.2.5) then sets each municipality’s share (quote-part) and the rates for each service. The agenda also lists the 2027 highlights and the framework and priorities for the Regions and Rurality Fund (FRR), whose 2025–2028 framework was dated March 18, 2026.

PressFor comparison, the regional council unanimously adopted its 2026 budget last November. The municipalities’ shares for 2026 totalled $4,448,391, or 46 per cent of the MRC’s revenue, according to the MRC’s statement as reported by The Gleaner.

Items 5.6 to 5.8 — the bus: a new by-law, a 2027 timetable and a 2027 fare schedule

Official documentThe MRC’s bus service runs three weekday lines. Its transport page describes the service as free for trips to or from a Haut-Saint-Laurent municipality, and says the on-demand taxibus has been suspended since December 31, 2024, because no carrier responded to the call for tenders.

AgendaThe September 16 agenda listed a notice of motion for a by-law repealing transit by-law 351-2025, a 2026 bus fare schedule and a September timetable (items 5.3 to 5.5). This agenda lists the adoption of By-law 361-2026, which repeals and replaces 351-2025, followed by a timetable and a fare schedule, both “à partir de janvier 2027”. The agenda does not say what the fare schedule contains.

MinutesThe MRC’s contract with Autobus La Québécoise covers 2026 and 2027 (resolution 11175-10-25). On August 19, the council approved two of its invoices, for June and July 2026, totalling $257,067.09 including taxes (resolution 11552-08-26). Item 6.4.1 is another payment to the same carrier.

Items 5.9 and 5.10 — heritage restoration aid

PressThe MRC adopted an inventory of buildings built before 1940 this year: 2,467 properties, 2,049 of them more than 100 years old (The Gleaner).

Official documentQuébec’s heritage agreement program funds, in sub-component 4.1, the restoration of privately owned heritage buildings; its table of eligible expenses says that from April 1, 2026, the inventory must be adopted by the MRC.

MinutesAgendaOn August 19, the council adopted the draft of By-law 358-2026 on a regional heritage restoration assistance program (resolution 11548-08-26). Its adoption was on the September 16 agenda (item 5.8). This agenda lists two items: renouncing the financial assistance proposed for sub-component 4.1 (item 5.9), and a notice of motion for a new program by-law that repeals 358-2026 (item 5.10). The agenda does not say why, or what the new program would offer owners.

Item 5.12 — wind energy

AgendaItem 5.12 is a notice of non-participation in Hydro-Québec’s call for tenders for wind energy.

ObserverAs the Observer reported on November 16, no wind project has been proposed in the MRC. Its 2006 wind by-law is still in force: a commercial turbine must stand at least 2 km from any home and 1 km from any public road, and the MRC says that while it is in force, no wind project can be established on its territory. The first-round deadline for bids in Hydro-Québec’s call, A/O 2026-01, is February 26, 2027; the tender document schedules later rounds in 2028 and 2030. The MRC’s survey of residents, run by Léger, collected answers from November 3 to 22, and the MRC expects to publish a proposed framework in January 2027, with written briefs in February and a public consultation in March. It calls those dates approximate.

Item 5.3 and item 6.5.5 — the regional land-use plan

Official documentThe MRC is revising its land-use and development plan. Its strategic vision statement, adopted on May 20, 2026, is to serve as the basis for that revision.

AgendaItem 5.3 starts a separate amendment to the plan in force, draft By-law 360-2026 on “diverses dispositions”: a notice of motion, the draft, a document stating the nature of the changes and a request for the minister’s opinion. Item 5.3.5 constitutes the public consultation commission for the revision of the plan. The agenda does not list the changes or the commission’s members.

MinutesOfficial documentItem 6.5.5 awards a contract for help drafting amendment 353-2025. The council adopted that draft in June 2025; it dealt with the “industrie” land-use designation (minutes of June 2025, item 5.3). Québec’s register of notified land-use documents lists 353-2025 as “Non conforme”, with a government notice dated September 5, 2025.

Item 5.1.4 — Ormstown’s subdivision amendment

ObserverAgendaWhen a municipality amends a planning by-law, the MRC gives its opinion on whether it conforms to the regional plan. Item 5.1.4 is that opinion for By-law 149.1-2026, which Ormstown council adopted on November 2. It amends subdivision by-law 149-2023 to exempt non-profits, schools and daycares, among others, from the contribution for parks. Items 5.1 and 5.2 list 11 other by-laws from Elgin, Franklin, Saint-Chrysostome, Très-Saint-Sacrement and Hinchinbrooke, and three minor variances from Saint-Anicet and Sainte-Barbe; the agenda does not describe them.

Items 6.6 and 6.7 — a regional by-law and elected officials’ pay

AgendaItem 6.6 adopts By-law 363-2026, a harmonized by-law enforced by the Sûreté du Québec, repealing By-law 352-2025, which the Observer explained here. The September 16 agenda had listed the adoption of By-law 359-2026, also repealing 352-2025 (item 6.4). The agenda does not say how 363-2026 differs.

AgendaItem 6.7 is a notice of motion for a new by-law on the pay of the MRC council’s members, repealing By-law 313-2020. The agenda gives no amounts.

Item 8.1 — the Droulers site

Official documentMinutesThe Droulers-Tsiionhiakwatha archaeological site in Saint-Anicet holds the remains of a St. Lawrence Iroquoian village from the mid-15th century; Québec classified it in 2017 (announcement). On August 19, the council granted up to $10,000 from the FRR to Les Aventuriers de l’archéologie dans le Haut-Saint-Laurent for a strategic plan for the interpretation centre, a $29,955 project (resolution 11559-08-26). Item 8.1 is an emphyteutic lease for the site: a long-term lease under which the tenant holds most of an owner’s rights and duties. The agenda does not name the parties or the term.

Other items

MinutesAgendaItem 5.13 updates how the municipalities share the cost of the West Branch of the La Guerre River in Saint-Anicet; in August, the council asked the CPTAQ to allow temporary storage on two lots during work to bring the pumping station on route 132 up to standard (resolution 11545-08-26). Items 5.15 and 5.16 grant aid to Franklin and Sainte-Barbe under the regional wetlands and water plan (PRMHH), adopted on October 15, 2025 (resolution 11167-10-25). Item 5.14 tables an accountability report from Ambition Habitation, the non-profit behind a 50-unit affordable housing project in Salaberry-de-Valleyfield. Item 6.5 lists agreements with the TCRM and Concertation Horizon, the external auditor’s contract and an organics collection contract for Howick. Item 8.2 is a partnership agreement for the Circuit du paysan. Item 6.3 sets the 2027 calendar of sittings.

AgendaPressThe notice of this sitting is signed by Maude Laberge, as director general and clerk-treasurer. Pierre Caza left the post this summer after five years (The Gleaner). Item 7 lists the appointment of a fire prevention technician and a retirement notice.

What to watch on November 25

  • Ormstown’s 2027 share under By-law 362-2026, and the budget parts it pays into.
  • What the 2027 bus fare schedule contains.
  • The reason given for renouncing the provincial heritage aid, and what the new program will offer.
  • The wording of the notice of non-participation in the wind call for tenders.
  • Who sits on the land-use plan’s public consultation commission.

How to take part

The sitting is public. It begins at 2:30 p.m. in the Council Room, office 400, 10 rue King, Huntingdon. There are two question periods: a general one at the start (item 3) and one at the end limited to agenda items (item 11).
