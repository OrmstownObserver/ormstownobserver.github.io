Urgent sitting on the fires: council presses Québec for more police

Mayor credits firefighters with saving the main street · Council unanimously asks Québec for lasting police resources · Neighbouring towns invited to back the call

The Ormstown Observer · Published November 11, 2026 · 5 p.m., hôtel de ville, 5 rue Gale
Observer Notes · Unofficial summary — official minutes pending
Community seriesOrmstown’s string of suspicious fires: what is known so far

Ormstown council met in a special sitting on Remembrance Day evening with one item on the agenda: the series of fires on rue Lambton. Mayor Philippe Besombes used most of the hour to thank the people who fought them, explain what he has asked of Québec, and read the resolution council then adopted unanimously.

“If the main street is still standing, it’s thanks to you”

Three officers of the Ormstown fire department attended. The mayor addressed them directly.

« Nos pompiers ont été exemplaires. Je pense que si la rue principale est encore debout, c’est grâce à vous. »

“Our firefighters were exemplary. I think that if the main street is still standing, it’s thanks to you.” — Mayor Philippe Besombes

The room gave the firefighters a standing ovation.

The mayor said he spent time at the scene and saw that several firefighters were shaken. Two families lived above the building that burned, he noted, so there was reason to be. But he told them they could be proud of their work and their crews. He also thanked the departments called in for mutual aid: Franklin, Saint-Étienne-de-Beauharnois, Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague, Saint-Stanislas-de-Kostka and Huntingdon. That help, he said, is why no one was hurt or killed and why neighbouring buildings were protected.

The mayor also thanked merchants and residents for their resilience and for the messages of support he has received, from Ormstown, from across Québec and beyond. He noted the fires drew national coverage, including an interview he gave to CBC’s As It Happens. He thanked his council colleagues for their support.

What the mayor has asked for

The mayor recounted the past few days. He went to the scene on Sunday. The discouragement was real, he said, but as mayor it had to be short, because it was time to act. That same day (Sunday, November 8), he called MNA Frédéric Laplante and told him that this could not continue. On Monday morning, he and councillors René Gauthier and Jane Fairhurst, who sit on the town’s civil security committee, met Québec’s public security minister, who came to Ormstown, along with senior Sûreté du Québec officers and the MNA.

« Le temps n’était plus aux paroles, le temps était aux actions. »“The time for words was over; it was time for action.” — Mayor Philippe Besombes

The minister asked the SQ to present a plan. Within days, the mayor said, the SQ’s director general called him with one: more police officers, day and night, dedicated to Ormstown. The mayor stressed the word dedicated, since the regional SQ post covers 13 municipalities.

He said he has no information on the investigation and won’t comment on it. That is the SQ’s work. Council’s role is to make sure the resources are there, and to ask for more if they’re needed. He also said he has met merchants on the street individually and will hold a meeting with them next week.

The resolution

The mayor read the resolution in full, saying “everything in it is important.” Its aim, he said, was to put on the record what has been done in recent days. In it, council:

  • states that the situation is unacceptable and that residents, merchants and families have a right to a safe place to live;
  • thanks the firefighters, first responders, the SQ and everyone who helped;
  • asks the Québec government, the public security minister and the MNA to recognize that the situation is urgent;
  • asks for additional police resources in Ormstown, with the SQ, to support the investigation and increase patrols for as long as needed;
  • asks that the SQ have every tool it needs to advance the investigations and prevent further fires;
  • asks for a regular line of communication between the town, the SQ and the public security ministry;
  • asks Québec to consider support for merchants and residents affected, including for the town’s economic recovery;
  • asks that preventive surveillance and security measures be discussed quickly with partners;
  • asks the ministry to assess medium- and long-term police needs in the area, including permanent SQ resources. “For now, we’re in temporary measures,” the mayor said;
  • calls for a follow-up meeting with the ministry, the SQ and the MNA;
  • mandates the mayor and the administration to keep pressing the case;
  • sends the resolution to the minister, the MNA, the SQ and the MRC du Haut-Saint-Laurent, makes it public, and invites the MRC’s 13 municipalities and those of neighbouring MRCs to adopt resolutions of support.

No one voted against. The resolution was adopted unanimously.

Question period

One resident asked whether cameras have been installed downtown. The mayor said he couldn’t give details, but that cameras are “not an issue”: many already exist, and the SQ has not flagged them as a need.

The sitting was recorded but not livestreamed. The mayor said the video will be posted on the Town’s YouTube channel.

What we don’t know yet

  • How many officers the SQ plan adds, and for how long.
  • When the adopted resolution will be published.
  • Which neighbouring municipalities will adopt a resolution of support.
  • The date of the follow-up meeting with the ministry and the SQ.

Anyone with information about the fires should contact the Sûreté du Québec (Centrale de l’information criminelle: 1-800-659-4264).
