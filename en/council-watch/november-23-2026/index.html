Council wrap-up: Nov. 23 special sitting

Director general suspended with pay · Interim DG named for up to five months · Outside firm hired to investigate and to review the workplace

The Ormstown Observer · Published November 23, 2026 · 5:30 p.m., hôtel de ville, 5 rue Gale
Observer Notes · Unofficial summary — official minutes pending
Disclosure. The Ormstown Observer's publisher, Jesse Roskies, is in litigation with the Municipality, and the director general is a witness for the Municipality in that case. He also asked questions during this sitting's question period. This account is based on the Observer's own record of the public sitting. Amounts and names will be checked against the official minutes when they are published.

Ormstown council met for an extraordinary session on Monday evening at town hall. All six councillors were present, with Mayor Philippe Besombes presiding. Council temporarily removed the director general from his duties, named an interim replacement, and hired an outside firm for two mandates.

Director general suspended

Council adopted what the agenda called a “temporary administrative measure” at the director general’s office. The resolution read by the mayor names Daniel Leduc, director general since July 22, 2024.

It says that, in light of circumstances that will be kept confidential, council considers it appropriate that the director general be temporarily removed from his duties. He keeps his pay and benefits. The measure took effect on November 19 and lasts until council decides otherwise.

No reason was given. The resolution, as read, says council wishes to preserve the director general’s rights. A suspension with pay is an administrative measure; it is not a finding against anyone. No councillor voted against.

Pierre Charron is interim director general

Council named Pierre Charron interim director general as of November 24, for a maximum of five months, unless council decides otherwise. He will hold all of the director general’s functions and powers during this time, and becomes an authorized signatory and principal administrator at Desjardins and at the other institutions where the Town holds accounts.

His pay is capped at $55,000 before tax for the five months, plus applicable benefits, under a contract the mayor is authorized to sign. The resolution did not say where Mr. Charron comes from or what his background is.

One firm, two mandates: a workplace review and an investigation

Workplace review, $6,000 plus tax. Council hired Synergie Gestion PME inc. to prepare a “bilan social”: an outside look at the work climate, management practices, the organization of work, professional relations, staff engagement and psychological health. Staff will be consulted and the firm will produce a report. The mayor said two prices were requested, as the Town’s new contract-management bylaw requires: Synergie at $6,000 plus tax, and Impact Partenaire d’affaires at $10,500 to $13,125 plus tax. The human resources director signs the contract.

Investigation, $7,200 plus tax. Council gave the same firm a mandate for an “independent administrative investigation” in what the resolution calls a specific human-resources management file. The resolution says the work must respect impartiality, independence and confidentiality. Two prices were received: Synergie at $7,200 plus tax and Impact Partenaire d’affaires at $7,400 plus tax. The mayor described Synergie’s offer, dated November 20, as the most advantageous. The mayor signs the contract.

The resolution does not say what or whom the investigation concerns. Asked during the question period, the mayor said he could not say more than the resolutions do, because employee files are confidential.

$8,000 more for the events contract

Council added $8,000 before tax to the contract it gave Groupe Charlebois in December 2025, for extras at the Noël enchanté on December 4. One councillor voted against. The Observer looks at that decision, and at what the Town has paid for its celebrations this year, in a separate story.

Also decided

  • Public Works. Three temporary, full-time, unionized posts for the length of certain employees’ absences: two labourers and one specialized labourer, the last as a pilot project. Two candidates were named, subject to psychometric tests.
  • Bank signatures. The human resources director, Kathy Laviolette, becomes principal administrator of the Town’s Desjardins services and an additional signatory. The mayor explained that every cheque needs two signatures and that only he and the director general could sign until now.

Question period

A resident asked what the investigation is about, who is conducting it, and whether forensic accountants will examine the Town’s books. The mayor answered that the matter is confidential, and that the Town’s accounts are examined every year by an external auditor, as the law requires. He said the audit checks whether each expense was authorized, by resolution or under the administration’s delegated power, and that the report is tabled publicly each spring. Asked why an audit would not have caught a problem, he said it was probably because there was no financial problem.

The same resident asked about adding the human resources director as a signatory. The mayor said three people can now sign, himself, the director general and the human resources director, and that he signs every cheque himself.

The Observer’s publisher asked about the events contract; the answers are in the separate story.

What we don’t know yet

  • Why the measure was taken, and how long it will last.
  • Who Pierre Charron is, and the terms of his contract.
  • What the investigation covers, when it is due, and whether its conclusions will be made public.
  • When the minutes and the Town’s video will be published.
