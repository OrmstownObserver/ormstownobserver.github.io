Regular sittingMonday, May 4, 2026

Ormstown council asks Quebec to ban trucks on three village streets

Council also agreed to more than triple its municipal line of credit on the only divided vote of the night, and promised, mid-meeting, to check whether a $200 estate-transfer charge billed to a recently widowed resident is applied fairly.

The Ormstown Observer · Published October 15, 2026 · Watch the full recording on YouTube →

Ormstown council voted Monday, May 4, to ask Quebec’s Ministry of Transport and Sustainable Mobility (MTMD) for authorization to install truck-restriction signage where Route 138 meets Roy, Church and Bridge streets. Council said several residents have reported serious truck and heavy-vehicle traffic problems in the village core, raising concerns for road safety, residential quiet and the condition of infrastructure (as read into the record). If Quebec approves, local deliveries would be exempted, and the ministry would work with the municipality on sign placement and specifications (as read). The public works director was authorized to pursue the request with the ministry (as read).

In its only non-unanimous vote of the evening, council agreed to seek an increase in its line of credit at the Caisse Desjardins du Haut-Saint-Laurent — from a current ceiling of $100,000 to as much as $1.6 million (as read). The mayor described the increase as short-term borrowing room, not new spending, made necessary by delays on major capital works — he cited one project, whose name the automated transcript did not render clearly, as an example. One councillor voted against the increase; the recording does not make clear which one (as heard). Earlier, council also approved a statement of financial operations showing the credit line drawn against that same project stood at $378,826.66 as of the meeting date (as read).

Council adopted several other items without dissent. It approved a new policy to prevent and address psychological harassment in the workplace, directing the director general to implement, communicate and enforce it (as read). It awarded an animal-control services contract to Contrôle animal Vaudreuil–Soulanges after reviewing submitted bids, following the municipality’s earlier termination of its previous contract, and citing by-law 131-2026 adopted at the last sitting (as read). Council authorized the director general and mayor to sign a tripartite funding agreement with the Office d’habitation du Haut-Saint-Laurent under Quebec’s PRHLM low-rent-housing renovation program (as read). It also adopted a revised code of ethics and conduct for elected officials, by-law 74-2026, mandatory following a general election (as read), and adopted a library by-law update, 90.1-2026, moving fee provisions into the municipality’s separate tariff by-law and clarifying non-resident membership rules (as read).

Council gave notice of motion on a draft by-law, numbered 167.1-2026, amending how municipal notices are published; no vote was required at this stage (as read). A separate tariff by-law covering municipal services, numbered 39-2026, was withdrawn from the agenda and will return at a future meeting (as read).

On community funding, council adopted a revised logistics-support policy for community events, catching and correcting a drafting error that would have set the effective date a month earlier than intended — the mayor flagged it live and the date was fixed at May 4, 2026 (as read). It also adopted a new policy on recognizing and supporting community organizations, dropping a requirement that applicant groups have a municipal resident on their board in favour of a requirement that they simply serve municipal residents, and requiring unregistered groups to apply through a registered non-profit acting as mandatary (as read). A related financial-assistance, partnership and bursary policy, replacing the municipality’s donations-and-sponsorship policy, was adopted as well (as read). Separately, council approved exceptional parking on Hector Street for FADOQ seniors’ club activities on six dates between May and December 2026, and asked the Sûreté du Québec to show leniency on parking enforcement during those windows (as read).

Question period

At the meeting’s opening 30-minute general question period, a resident thanked council for supporting a local cookie fundraiser (as heard). A second resident told council that their spouse died in October 2025 and that they were later billed $200 for a supplementary property-transfer duty on the home the couple built and paid for together. The resident argued the charge is discretionary rather than mandatory, and said their understanding is that some other municipalities waive it when a surviving spouse inherits jointly held property (as heard). The director general said the duty is legal and applied by most Quebec municipalities, but that a municipality that applies it must do so consistently, without discriminating between types of transfers — for instance, applying it to transfers between children but not between spouses (as heard). A councillor identified in the room as a notary made a similar point about the duty’s legality (as heard). The mayor and director general committed to check when and how the duty was adopted in Ormstown, verify how it was applied in this case, and raise the question with other mayors at the MRC (as heard).

In the second question period, restricted to items on the day’s agenda, a resident asked why council was re-adopting library rules on non-resident membership that already existed under the prior by-law (as heard). Council said the text needed updating because staff and volunteers were unclear on the rule, and because the previous wording incorrectly implied non-residents could subscribe for a flat $25 fee — in fact, non-residents may only join if their home municipality belongs to the Réseau Biblio network (as heard). Council said it is looking at whether more neighbouring municipalities could join that network to expand access (as heard).
