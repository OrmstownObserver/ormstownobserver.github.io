Council adds $8,000 to an events contract whose resolution set no amount

What Ormstown has paid outside firms for its public celebrations in 2026, what the mayor says the budget was, and why the numbers do not yet line up

The Ormstown Observer · Published November 23, 2026 · 5:30 p.m., hôtel de ville, 5 rue Gale
Observer Notes · Unofficial summary — official minutes pending
Full recap of this sittingCouncil wrap-up: Nov. 23 special sitting
Disclosure. The Ormstown Observer's publisher, Jesse Roskies, is in litigation with the Municipality, and the director general is a witness for the Municipality in that case. He also asked questions during this sitting's question period. This account is based on the Observer's own audio recording of the public sitting. Amounts and names will be checked against the official minutes when they are published. The publisher also organizes events and produces media through his own businesses, and is president of the Ormstown Soccer Club.

At Monday’s special sitting, Ormstown council approved $8,000 more, before tax, for the firm it hired to help run the Town’s big public events. One councillor voted against. The original mandate, voted eleven months ago, named no price. Here is what the public record shows about what has been paid, step by step.

What council decided on Monday

The mayor explained that Groupe Charlebois is organizing this year’s Noël enchanté, on December 4 from 4 p.m. After the fires on rue Lambton, and a meeting with merchants a few days ago, council wants something more elaborate than planned. The added $8,000 would pay for extras such as a horse-drawn carriage and decorations, including a decorated screen to hide the site of the restaurant that burned, if that proves possible.

Council approved the spending. One councillor said “contre” when the vote was called.

The paper trail

1. December 17, 2025: a mandate with no amount. Weeks after the new council took office, it awarded Groupe Charlebois a mandate to help plan and run “certain large-scale municipal events” in 2026, “notably” the Fête nationale and Canada Day (resolution 25-12-377). The resolution says two organizations were approached, Groupe Charlebois and Une Affaire de Famille, and calls Groupe Charlebois “the only supplier able to carry out the mandate.” It states no price and no budget. It leaves the final agreement to the director general and the recreation coordinator. Minutes, Dec. 17, 2025

« D’OCTROYER au Groupe Charlebois un mandat d’accompagnement pour la planification et la gestion logistique de certains événements municipaux de grande envergure pour l’année 2026; D’IMPUTER la dépense au poste budgétaire approprié; D’AUTORISER la direction générale et la responsable des loisirs à entreprendre les démarches requises pour finaliser l’entente avec le Groupe Charlebois. »Resolution 25-12-377, in full: a mandate to help plan and manage the logistics of certain large-scale municipal events in 2026; the expense charged to “the appropriate budget line”; the administration authorized to finalize the agreement.

The resolution names the Fête nationale and Canada Day. On Monday the mayor said the mandate covered Canada Day, the family festival and the Noël enchanté, and that the Fête nationale was organized by the Town itself.

2. June 1, 2026: $29,893.50. The payment list approved that night shows a payment to Groupe Charlebois labelled “1er vers 50%”, a first instalment of 50 percent. It sits in the block of payments already issued. Minutes, June 1, 2026, Annexe A

3. August 3, 2026: $5,978.70. A second payment, labelled “2e vers 20%”. Minutes, Aug. 3, 2026, Annexe A

4. November 23, 2026: $8,000 more, before tax. Monday’s decision.

What the mayor says the budget was

During the question period, the mayor said that what council agreed to last December was a budget of $34,000 for Groupe Charlebois, covering Canada Day, the family festival and the Noël enchanté, and that he considers the contract still within that figure. He added that he could verify, but that those were the numbers he had.

The record does not yet match that figure, for two reasons.

  • The resolution contains no amount, so $34,000 appears in no public document the Observer has found.
  • The first payment alone, labelled as 50 percent, was $26,000 before tax. Half of a $34,000 contract would be $17,000. If $26,000 was half, the contract would be $52,000 before tax.

Where $60,000 comes from. The Town’s own payment list calls $29,893.50 a first instalment of 50 percent. Double it and the contract is $59,787.00 with tax, or $52,000 before tax. The second payment, $5,978.70, is exactly 10 percent of that total, although the list labels it 20 percent. On those figures, $23,914.80 would remain to be paid, before Monday’s $8,000.

That is the figure the publisher put to the mayor at the microphone. He also said “almost $100,000”: if the Groupe Charlebois contract is $59,787.00, the Town’s 2026 event suppliers come to $89,031.75 once Monday’s addition and the Fête nationale suppliers below are counted. What has actually been paid so far is less, and is set out in the tables on this page.

There may be a simple explanation for the gap between $34,000 and the payment labels, such as a label entered wrongly or a budget counted differently. The signed agreement would settle it. It has not been made public.

The Fête nationale: a second supplier, no resolution found

Groupe Charlebois was not the only outside supplier this year. For the Fête nationale, the Town paid Robmusic $19,011.98 in two payments, and $1,034.77 for inflatable games. The Observer found no resolution naming Robmusic in the minutes of 2025 or 2026.

That does not by itself mean a rule was broken. As the mayor said on Monday, the payment list includes spending that council voted and spending the administration authorized on its own: under bylaw 130-2023, the director general can approve purchases up to $25,000 before tax without a vote. The mayor said the Fête nationale was organized by the Town with the help of local non-profits, that it receives special grants, and that he did not have its final accounts with him.

In all, the Town has paid $55,918.95 to three event suppliers in 2026, and council has not voted an amount for any of them. With Monday’s addition, the total committed reaches $65,116.95.

What residents said

The question period turned on this point. The publisher asked why the Town pays outside firms for events that local volunteers organized for years. The mayor answered that when council was elected there was no longer a Noël enchanté, that nothing had been happening for some time, and that council wanted to give the Town’s festivities a structure.

The Observer’s view

This section is opinion.

Three things about this file should concern residents, whatever they think of the events themselves. A mandate was awarded with no amount, so the public had no way to know what it would cost. Payments were issued before council saw them on a list. And the money is leaving town: a firm from elsewhere is paid tens of thousands of dollars for work that, residents say, Ormstown’s own volunteers did for years.

Each of these has a simple fix. Put the amount in the resolution. Publish the agreement. And before the 2027 budget is adopted, ask Ormstown’s organizations what they could do with the same money.

What we don’t know yet

  • The total value of the Groupe Charlebois agreement, and what the remaining payments will be.
  • Why the first payment was labelled 50 percent.
  • Who authorized the Robmusic contract, and what grants offset the Fête nationale.
  • What the September 26 citizens’ festival cost and who was paid; no payment for it appears on the lists published so far.
