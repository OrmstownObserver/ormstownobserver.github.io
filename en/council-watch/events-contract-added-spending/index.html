From $12,500 to $56,000: what Ormstown now pays contractors to run its celebrations

On Monday council added $8,000 more. Most of the money goes to an events firm from outside town, under a resolution that set no amount. The documents, for residents and for council.

The Ormstown Observer · Published November 23, 2026 · 5:30 p.m., hôtel de ville, 5 rue Gale

Two summers ago, Ormstown booked the entertainment for its Fête nationale itself, one act at a time, for about $12,500. This year the Town has paid outside contractors almost $56,000 to stage its public celebrations, most of it to an events firm from out of town. On Monday council added $8,000 more.

Paid to date

  • Groupe Charlebois, an events firm from outside Ormstown, hired in December 2025 to help organize the Town’s large events. Two payments so far: $29,893.50 and $5,978.70.
  • Robmusic, entertainment for the Fête nationale: $19,011.98 in two payments. The Observer found no council resolution naming this supplier.
  • Inflatable games for the Fête nationale: $1,034.77. No resolution found.

How it was done before

In 2025 there was no outside organizer. The Town’s own payment lists show it hiring each act and vendor for the Fête nationale directly: a singer, Robmusic ($4,236.83); a stage show ($4,886.44); an entertainment act ($1,235.98); a cotton-candy and popcorn stand ($549.60); and inflatable games ($1,626.90). Five suppliers, each on its own line.

In 2026 the lists show one line for entertainment: Robmusic, $19,011.98, described only as “Fête Nationale du Québec - 2026”. No band or other act is listed separately, so the lists do not show which performers that amount covered. The Observer searched every payment list from May to October 2026 and found no other performer paid for the Fête nationale, Canada Day or the September festival.

Other celebrations ran on volunteers. Residents say a committee of townspeople organized the Noël enchanté for years. Asked on Monday why the Town now pays an outside firm for events local volunteers used to run, the mayor answered that when this council was elected there was no longer a Noël enchanté, that nothing had been happening for some time, and that council wanted to give the Town’s festivities a structure.

What council voted in December

On December 17, 2025, weeks after taking office, council awarded Groupe Charlebois its mandate (resolution 25-12-377). The resolution says two organizations were approached, Groupe Charlebois and the local group Une Affaire de Famille, and calls Groupe Charlebois “the only supplier able to carry out the mandate.” This is everything council voted:

« D’OCTROYER au Groupe Charlebois un mandat d’accompagnement pour la planification et la gestion logistique de certains événements municipaux de grande envergure pour l’année 2026; D’IMPUTER la dépense au poste budgétaire approprié; D’AUTORISER la direction générale et la responsable des loisirs à entreprendre les démarches requises pour finaliser l’entente avec le Groupe Charlebois. »Resolution 25-12-377: a mandate to help plan and manage the logistics of certain large municipal events in 2026; the cost charged to “the appropriate budget line”; the administration authorized to finalize the agreement. Minutes, Dec. 17, 2025

No price and no ceiling. The only events named are the Fête nationale and Canada Day, introduced by “notamment” (notably). The agreement that followed has not been made public.

Two sets of numbers

On Monday the mayor said council agreed last December on a budget of $34,000 for Groupe Charlebois, covering Canada Day, the family festival and the Noël enchanté, and that the contract is still within it. He said he could verify, but that those were the figures he had.

The Town’s payment lists point to a different figure:

  • The June 1 payment list calls the first payment, $29,893.50, a first instalment of 50 percent. Double it and the contract is $59,787.00 with tax, or $52,000 before tax.
  • The August 3 list calls the second payment, $5,978.70, an instalment of 20 percent. It is exactly 10 percent of $59,787.
  • The two payments already come to $31,200 before tax. Add Monday’s $8,000 and the total passes $34,000.
  • The resolution names the Fête nationale and Canada Day. The mayor said the Fête nationale was run by the Town itself.

Both payments appear in the block of payments already issued when council approved the lists. There may be a simple explanation, such as a label entered wrongly. The signed agreement would settle it.

What council added on Monday

Groupe Charlebois is organizing the Noël enchanté on December 4, from 4 p.m. The mayor said that after the fires on rue Lambton, and a meeting with merchants, council wants something more elaborate than planned. The added $8,000, before tax, would pay for extras such as a horse-drawn carriage and decorations, including a decorated screen to hide the site of the restaurant that burned, if that proves possible. The September 26 citizens’ festival at Parc Lindsay-Cullen is the least documented. The Observer was there and saw an entertainment company, Animation Concept, running the games, the music and the hosting. No resolution about the festival appears in the minutes from August to October, and no payment for it is on the lists published so far. It may have been paid through Groupe Charlebois, or its bill may be on a list not yet published.

A missing resolution does not by itself mean a rule was broken. As the mayor noted on Monday, the payment lists include spending the administration authorized on its own: under bylaw 130-2023, the director general can approve purchases up to $25,000 before tax without a vote. The Town’s new contract-management bylaw sets the rules for how such contracts are awarded. The mayor also said the Fête nationale receives special grants and was organized with local non-profits, and that he did not have its final accounts with him.

What this adds up to

Council set out to bring Ormstown’s celebrations back, and it has: Canada Day and a family festival were held this year, and the Noël enchanté returns on December 4. The question the documents raise is not whether to hold these events, but what they cost and who decides.

The December resolution left the price, the list of events and the terms to be settled afterward by the administration. Because of that, neither residents nor, judging by Monday’s exchange, council members had one place to see the total. The figures on this page had to be assembled from payment lists published months apart.

Questions council could put to the administration

  • What is the total value of the signed agreement with Groupe Charlebois, which events does it cover, and what remains to be paid?
  • Why was the first payment labelled 50 percent?
  • Did Une Affaire de Famille, or any other local group, give a price for the 2026 mandate?
  • Who authorized the Robmusic contract, which performers did its $19,011.98 cover, and what grants offset the cost of the Fête nationale?
  • What did the September 26 festival cost, and who was paid?
  • What did each 2026 event cost in total, and what did it cost the year before?

The Observer’s view

This section is opinion.

None of this requires anyone to have acted in bad faith. An open-ended resolution produces exactly this result: spending that is legal, approved after the fact, and hard for anyone, council included, to add up. Three practical steps would fix it. Put an amount, or a ceiling, in the resolution. Publish the agreement. And before the 2027 budget is adopted, set a budget for each major event and ask Ormstown’s own organizations and volunteers what part of it they could take on.
