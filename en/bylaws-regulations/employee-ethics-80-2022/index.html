Council & Ethics · Bylaw 80-2022

Gifts, conflicts of interest, confidentiality: the rules Ormstown's municipal staff must follow

Bylaw 80-2022, in force since May 4, 2022, is the code of ethics and conduct for the town's employees. A breach can lead to a sanction, from a verbal notice up to dismissal.

The Ormstown Observer · Bylaws & Regulations Explainer

Key takeaway

  • Employees may not accept any gift, hospitality or other benefit offered by a supplier (art. 7.3.5).
  • A possible conflict of interest must be reported to the immediate supervisor (art. 7.4).
  • Staff need the director general's prior authorization to publish a text or give an interview about their duties or the municipality's activities (art. 12).
  • Staff involved in tenders or contracts face a one-year limit on working for a municipal bidder or supplier after leaving (art. 7.4).
  • Sanctions range from a verbal notice to dismissal (art. 13.2).

Why this bylaw exists

The Loi sur l'éthique et la déontologie en matière municipale (chapter E-15.1.0.1) requires municipalities to adopt a code of ethics and conduct for their employees, and Bill 49, assented to November 5, 2021, required the code to be revised by May 5, 2022 (preamble).

Notice of motion was given and the draft tabled April 4, 2022, with public notice of the draft April 5. Council adopted the bylaw on May 2, 2022, unanimously among the members present. Public notice of adoption was given May 4, 2022, the date the cover page gives for entry into force; article 15 says the bylaw takes effect on the day of its publication. It replaces and repeals any earlier code adopted by the municipality, without naming one (art. 14).

Who it covers

Managers and employees, plus volunteers and council members who sit on municipal committees, such as the planning advisory committee (CCU) (art. 4).

Conflicts of interest and gifts

Employees may not favour their personal interests in their work (art. 7.3.1), or accept any gift or benefit, whatever its value, that could influence their judgment (art. 7.3.4).

An employee who believes they are, or may be, in a conflict of interest must inform their immediate supervisor; ultimately, the director general tells them how to act. Staff must also (art. 7.4):

  • not handle the file of a family member, someone close or a friend, and tell the immediate supervisor at once if given one;
  • refuse invitations to events such as a golf tournament or a hockey game, unless the municipality pays for the tickets.

Information and resources

No one covered by the code may use or communicate confidential or personal information learned through their work, or consult it unless their duties require it (art. 12). Municipal resources may not be used for personal purposes, subject to any specific policy on their use (art. 11).

After leaving the job

Staff who took part directly in a tender process or awarded contracts may not, for one year after leaving, hold a decision-making, contract-negotiation or representation role with a bidder or supplier of the municipality (art. 7.4). In the 12 months after the end of their mandate (fin de leur mandat), the director general, treasurer, clerk, their deputies and any other senior employee designated by council may not take a job or a director's or officer's post that lets them or anyone else draw an undue advantage from their former duties (art. 8 b).

Sanctions

Managers report breaches to the director general (art. 8 a). The municipality decides the sanction, in compliance with any employment contract (art. 13), in proportion to the seriousness of the breach and the person's degree of responsibility (art. 13.1): a verbal notice, a written notice, a reprimand, a suspension without pay, or dismissal (art. 13.2). The code sets no fines.

What this means for residents

Municipal employees may not accept gifts, hospitality or other benefits offered by a supplier (art. 7.3.5), and must report a possible conflict of interest to their immediate supervisor (art. 7.4).
