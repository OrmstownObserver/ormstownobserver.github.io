Zoning & Land Use · Bylaw 149-2023

Subdividing land in Ormstown: a permit, minimum lot sizes and 10% for parks

Bylaw 149-2023 applies to every cadastral operation in Ormstown. It sets minimum lot sizes, standards for new streets, and a contribution of 10% of the land or its value for parks. The copy the town has posted is an administrative consolidation without official value, with no adoption or in-force date. A first draft amendment, 149.1-2026, would add seven exemptions to the parks contribution.

Key takeaway

  • Any cadastral operation, with or without streets, needs a subdivision permit (permis de lotissement) before it is carried out (art. 1.3, 2.3).
  • Unless article 4.2 exempts the operation, the owner must give the town land or money worth 10% of the site's area or value for parks before a plan is approved. Council chooses which (art. 4.1, 4.2, 4.3).
  • Outside a riparian corridor, a lot without water or sewer needs 2,800 m² and 45 m of width; on the shore or within 100 m of a designated watercourse, it needs 3,700 m² (art. 3.17).
  • A first offence carries a fine of at least $800 and at most $1,000 for an individual, and at most $2,000 for a company, for each day (art. 2.18).

Why this bylaw exists

Article 1.2 says the bylaw gives the municipality the legal means to regulate cadastral operations on its territory. It replaces Bylaw 23-2006 (art. 1.4).

The town's copy is an administrative consolidation (codification administrative) that says it “has no official value”. Its update date and table of amendments are blank, and it prints no notice of motion, adoption or in-force date; article 6.1 says it comes into force “in accordance with the law”.

Getting a subdivision permit

  1. A surveyor draws the planIt must be prepared by a land surveyor (arpenteur-géomètre) (art. 2.6).
  2. ConditionsThe owner pays any overdue municipal taxes on the property (art. 2.5), transfers land for future public streets by notarized deed at the owner's expense (art. 2.4), and makes the parks contribution (art. 4.1).
  3. ReviewAn incomplete application will not be processed (art. 2.7). Any change to the plans after approval needs a new application and the fee (art. 2.9).
  4. Registration6 months (art. 2.12) or 12 months (art. 2.10)Article 2.12 says the permit lapses if the plan is not registered with the ministère des Ressources naturelles within six months of the permit's issue. Article 2.10, on opening a new street, says the applicant must register the subdivision within twelve months of the permit's issue.

Under the fee schedule, Bylaw 39-2026 (in force June 1, 2026), the permit costs $100 plus $50 per lot added (item 60).

Minimum lot sizes

For serviced lots, minimums are set zone by zone in the zoning bylaw's grids (art. 3.16). Under the permits bylaw, a lot is serviced when water and sanitary sewer are in the street along it, or a bylaw ordering their installation is in force, and partially serviced when only one is.

The parks contribution

The owner must give the town, free of charge, land council considers suitable for a park, playground or natural space, or pay money instead, or a mix (art. 4.1).

  • How much: 10% of the area or value of the whole site. For land already subdivided without any contribution, 10% of “a part” of the site; the bylaw does not say which part. A mix cannot exceed 10% of the land's value (art. 4.3).
  • Who chooses: council, in every case (art. 4.3).
  • Valuation: as of the date all documents are received, by a chartered appraiser hired by the town and paid by the owner (art. 4.4).

Exempt operations (art. 4.2):

  • cancelling, correcting or replacing lot numbers without adding lots;
  • land already subject to a contribution in the last 12 months;
  • agricultural purposes;
  • a separate lot for land already occupied by a main building;
  • a vertical cadastre for a condominium conversion;
  • merging parcels under article 3043 of the Civil Code;
  • re-identifying an already subdivided site after its limits change, without creating new building lots;
  • in renovated territory, returning to the pre-renovation number of lots, if park fees were paid;
  • the expropriated part, in an expropriation for public utility;
  • an operation to obtain a mortgage discharge (mainlevée), not for construction.

Draft amendment: Bylaw 149.1-2026

First draft bylaw 149.1-2026 would add paragraphs k) to q) to article 4.2, exempting: non-profits (OBNL or OSBL) offering free services to Ormstown residents and registered with the Registraire des entreprises du Québec for two years; transfers of ownership for public purposes; operations by the municipality; a transitional lot to be transferred to an adjoining property; operations resulting from a judgment or a boundary determination (bornage); establishing or expanding a preschool, primary or secondary school, a vocational training centre or an adult education centre; and licensed daycares and early childhood centres (CPE).

Its preamble says notice of motion was given on July 6, 2026. Council Watch reported that notice (July 6) and the first draft carried on August 3, 2026. On the posted copy, the later steps on the cover are blank, including the public consultation, the second draft, adoption and entry into force.

Streets

No new street may be opened in zones A and AD (art. 3.2). A cul-de-sac may run at most 150 m to a turning circle with an outer radius of at least 20 m, or 250 m if a drivable pedestrian and emergency path at least 5 m wide links it to a neighbouring street (art. 3.8).

Older lots and acquired rights

Minimum sizes do not apply to lots registered before the bylaw took effect (art. 5.1); the posted copy does not print that date (art. 6.1). A nonconforming lot has acquired rights if it met the rules in force when it was filed with the cadastre or when a complete permit application was filed. The owner must prove them (art. 5.2). Land that was not a separate lot on April 11, 1983 can still be subdivided in the cases in article 5.3. In the agricultural zone, a lot with acquired rights cannot be refused for width if a lane at least 5 m wide links it to a public road or a conforming private street (art. 5.6).

Penalties

  • First offence: for each day or part of a day, a fine of at least $800 and at most $1,000 for an individual, and at most $2,000 for a company (art. 2.18).
  • Repeat: $1,000 minimum, up to $2,000 for an individual or $4,000 for a company, plus costs (art. 2.18).
  • A subdivision made in breach of the bylaw can be annulled by the Superior Court at the town's request (art. 2.17).

What this means for residents

A cadastral operation needs a permit, on a land surveyor\'s plan (art. 2.3, 2.6). Tables 3.2 and 3.3 set minimum lot sizes by water and sewer service and by distance to water (art. 3.17). Unless article 4.2 applies, the owner gives 10% of the site\'s area or value, in land or money, as council chooses (art. 4.1, 4.3).
