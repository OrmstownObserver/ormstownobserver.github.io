Finance & Taxation · Bylaw 39-2026

What Ormstown charges, line by line: permits, planning requests, pet licences, hall rentals and more

Bylaw 39-2026, in force since June 1, 2026, is the municipality’s price list: two annexes of numbered lines, running from 1 to 87, from a $0.50 photocopy to a $2,000 request to change a planning bylaw. Every fee is payable before the service is delivered.

The Ormstown Observer · Bylaws & Regulations Explainer

Key takeaway

  • Every fee is payable to the municipality before the service is delivered or the activity begins. For works, the director of public works sets an estimate, which is paid before the work starts and then adjusted to the actual cost (art. 3).
  • Paying a fee does not exempt anyone from the obligations, conditions or procedures set by the municipality’s other bylaws or resolutions (art. 4).
  • The schedule replaces every tariff in any bylaw or resolution for the services it covers. It does not name the earlier texts it replaces (art. 5).
  • Building fees include $350 plus $150 per dwelling for a new home, $125 to renovate a residential principal building, $75 for a residential accessory building, $40 for a fence or hedge and $100 for a pool and patio (art. 1, annex A, lines 49, 52, 53, 69, 71).
  • A minor variance costs $650, non-refundable. A request to change a planning bylaw costs $2,000. $1,000 comes back if council refuses it, but not if the amending bylaw is withdrawn after a register or referendum with a negative result (art. 1, annex A, lines 61–62).

Why this bylaw exists

The preamble says the municipality wants to change the rates for the various services it offers. Article 1 imposes the rates, fees and prices listed in annex A on anyone who wants to use the property or services listed, or take part in the activities. Article 6 says it comes into force in accordance with the law.

Notice of motion was given at the ordinary sitting of January 19, 2026. The draft was tabled April 13, 2026. The bylaw was adopted June 1, 2026 and came into force the same day. It is signed by the mayor and by the director general and clerk-treasurer.

The rules that apply to every fee

  • What it replaces: any provision in a bylaw or resolution that sets a price for a property, service or activity covered here is replaced by the rates in Bylaw 39-2026 (art. 5).
  • Administration fee: where a line carries an asterisk, the administration fee is 15% (annex A, note under line 81).
  • Services with no set price: for a municipal service with no price in this or any other bylaw, and for damage to municipal property, the annex applies a formula: [hourly wage of each worker, increased by 30% + other costs incurred by the municipality x 1.10] (annex A, second line 32). Lines 33, 36, 37 and 45 use the same formula for some public works services.

The body of the bylaw has no general refund rule; refunds appear line by line in the annex. It contains no fines, and it does not say whether sales taxes apply to any amount.

Animals

What this means for residents

Every amount is payable in advance (art. 3). A new home’s permit fee comes with a separate $200 deposit, not refunded if the certificate of location is not produced after 2 years (line 49). For a request to change a planning bylaw, the $2,000 is due before the study begins, and $1,000 is refunded if council refuses the request (line 62). A minor variance fee is not refundable (line 61).
