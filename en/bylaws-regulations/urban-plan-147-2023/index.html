Urban Planning · Bylaw 147-2023

Ormstown's urban plan: 11 land-use designations that the zoning bylaw must respect

Bylaw 147-2023, the plan d'urbanisme, divides the territory into 11 land-use designations, sets minimum housing densities and says development is to be concentrated in the urban perimeter. It sets no fines; the zoning bylaw must respect it.

The copy the town has posted shows no adoption or in-force date. It does not include Bylaw 147.1-2025, which amended the plan in April 2026 and whose text the Observer has not seen.

The Ormstown Observer · Bylaws & Regulations Explainer

Key takeaway

  • The zoning bylaw must respect the plan's classification of uses and the uses it authorizes (art. 5.1.1).
  • 11 land-use designations, with minimum densities of 15 dwellings per hectare in R, 20 in RO and 3 in RFD, and 20 per hectare in the village core (art. 5.2.6, 5.2.9 to 5.2.11).
  • Development is to be concentrated inside the single urban perimeter; about 26 hectares added on the 3e rang must be developed in phases (art. 3.1.1.2, 3.1.1.3).
  • The protected agricultural zone covers 13,824 hectares, just over 96% of the territory (art. 3.4.1.1).
  • It replaces Bylaw 24-2006 and can be changed only by a bylaw adopted under the planning act and the Municipal Code (art. 1.1.3, 1.1.5).
  • Bylaw 147.1-2025, adopted April 13, 2026, amends the plan (147.1-2025 notices).

Why this bylaw exists

The foreword calls the revised plan "un instrument légal" meeting the requirements of Québec's planning act, the Loi sur l'aménagement et l'urbanisme (LAU), and says the town's plan and planning bylaws must agree with the MRC du Haut-Saint-Laurent's land-use plan (schéma d'aménagement). The plan covers the whole territory and replaces the "Règlement du Plan d'urbanisme n° 24-2006" and all its amendments (art. 1.1.2, 1.1.3).

The copy the town has posted gives no notice-of-motion, adoption or in-force date. Article 6.1 says the bylaw comes into force "conformément à la loi". The consolidation page leaves the table of amendments empty and says the consolidation "n'a aucune valeur officielle".

The town's notice of adoption, dated April 20, 2026, says council adopted "Règlement 147.1-2025 modifiant le règlement concernant le plan d'urbanisme 147-2023" at its ordinary sitting of April 13, 2026, and that it comes into force on the day the notice is published. The notice of public consultation, dated March 19, 2026, says the amendment modifies "certaines dispositions relatives à la politique sur la protection des rives, du littoral et des zones inondables et l'hébergement touristique de plein air lié à une entreprise agricole enregistrée" (provisions on the protection of shorelines, the littoral and flood zones, and on outdoor tourist lodging linked to a registered farm business). The passages of the plan on those two subjects are marked below.

How the plan applies to other bylaws

The plan sets no offences or fines. It says:

  • Each designation sets the main uses; the zoning bylaw must respect them, and specifies densities (art. 5.1.1, 5.1.2).
  • The lists of uses are not exhaustive: other uses compatible with the dominant vocation can be authorized (art. 1.2.2).
  • Designation boundaries can be adjusted in the zoning plan; only the urban perimeter and the permanent agricultural zone apply exactly as drawn (art. 1.2.3).
  • The plan creates "aucune obligation" as to when or how its proposed measures are carried out (art. 1.2.1).

The 11 land-use designations

A designation (grande affectation) assigns a use, function or vocation to a part of the territory. Densities are gross, in dwellings per hectare (art. 5.1, 5.1.2).

The uses listed above for A and AD are those of the plan as printed. The consultation notice says Bylaw 147.1-2025 modifies provisions on outdoor tourist lodging linked to a registered farm business.

Where growth is planned

Ormstown has one urban perimeter, the former village, plus two white zones: along the 3e rang, and on Route 201 at the Franklin boundary. Development is to be concentrated inside the perimeter (art. 3.1.1.2). The plan lists six sectors of 1 hectare or more (art. 3.1.1.3, 3.1.1.4):

Article 3.1.1.4 places the six sectors "à l'intérieur de son périmètre d'urbanisation" (inside its urban perimeter). Tables 4 and 5 list sectors 5 and 6 as white zones, and article 5.2.10 describes the RFD white zones as located outside the perimeter.

With an increase of about 242 households projected by 2041, the plan says the town's residential space needs to 2041 "pourront facilement être comblés" (art. 3.1.1.4).

About 26 hectares added to the perimeter on the 3e rang must be developed in phases: before a phase starts, 75% of the previous phase's residential building lots must have a house built or work carried out under a building permit issued by the town (art. 3.1.1.3). Plan 1-1 shows four phases of 6.6, 10, 4.8 and 4.2 hectares.

The plan's stated priorities

Chapter III sets ten orientations (art. 3):

  1. Encourage diversified, harmonious and accessible residential development.
  2. Preserve the integrity of existing built areas.
  3. Consolidate commerce and energize economic activity.
  4. Develop the Route 201 Sud industrial pole.
  5. Protect and enhance natural features of interest and prevent the harmful effects of urban heat islands.
  6. Showcase Ormstown's identity and heritage.
  7. Preserve and enhance farmland and farming.
  8. Guarantee quality services, facilities and infrastructure.
  9. Plan the different modes of transport harmoniously.
  10. Protect the public from natural and human-made constraints.

On shorelines and flood zones, the plan says the town is to apply the transitional regime in force since March 1, 2022 on shorelines, the littoral and the floodplain, along with the MRC's standards, and carry those rules into its planning bylaws (art. 3.3.1.1, 3.3.2, 3.6.2). The consultation notice says Bylaw 147.1-2025 modifies provisions on this policy.

What this means for residents

The plan sets no offences or fines. The zoning bylaw must respect its classification of uses and the uses it authorizes (art. 5.1.1). The Annex A maps that show where each designation applies are not in the posted copy (art. 5.1).
