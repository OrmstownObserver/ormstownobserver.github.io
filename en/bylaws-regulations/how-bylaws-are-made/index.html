From notice of motion to public notice: how Ormstown council makes a bylaw, passes a resolution, and where you fit in

Council decides everything by bylaw or by resolution. Bylaws go through at least two sittings and a public notice; resolutions take effect the night they pass. Here is each step, using draft Bylaw 129-2026 as the example.

The Ormstown Observer · Bylaws & Regulations Explainer

Key takeaway

  • A Québec municipality acts in only two ways: by bylaw (règlement) or by resolution (résolution).
  • A bylaw needs a notice of motion, a draft tabled in public, and adoption at a separate, later sitting. It takes effect when a public notice is published.
  • A resolution is voted at one sitting and takes effect right away, subject to the mayor's veto.
  • The window between the draft and the adoption vote is when residents can read the text and ask questions.

Two tools: bylaws and resolutions

Every decision council makes is either a bylaw or a resolution. A bylaw is used when a law requires one, usually for lasting rules that apply to everyone: zoning, taxes, nuisances, contracts, borrowing. A resolution is used for everything else: awarding a contract, approving the bills to pay, hiring, setting a meeting calendar, applying for a grant.

Both are decided in public at a council sitting. A sitting can proceed only if a majority of council members are present (the quorum, article 147 of the Code municipal du Québec). Ormstown's council holds a regular sitting at least once a month (art. 148 C.M.).

How a bylaw is made

Since 2018, article 445 of the Municipal Code sets the steps, as the Québec government's Muni-Express bulletin explains:

  1. Notice of motion (avis de motion)A council member announces that a bylaw will be adopted at a later sitting.129-2026: October 6, 2026
  2. Draft tabled and presented (dépôt du projet)A council member tables the draft. This can happen before, after or at the same sitting as the notice. Copies must be made available to the public as soon as possible.129-2026: October 6, 2026; copies available at the sitting
  3. Adoption at a separate sittingThe bylaw is adopted at a sitting distinct from the one where the draft was tabled. It no longer has to be read aloud, but its object, scope, cost and any financing must be stated, and any changes since the draft must be mentioned. Copies of the version being adopted must be available from the start of that sitting.129-2026: not yet adopted
  4. Signature and public noticeThe mayor and clerk sign it, and a public notice of adoption is posted.129-2026: pending
  5. Entry into forceThe bylaw takes effect on the day the public notice is published, unless a law sets another date.129-2026: pending

Changes between the draft and adoption are allowed, but they cannot change the bylaw's object. A bigger change means starting over with a new draft.

Some bylaws need extra steps

Certain bylaws go further before they can take effect. Some zoning and urban-planning changes and most borrowing bylaws must be submitted to eligible voters. Typically a register is opened where residents can sign to ask for a referendum, and urban-planning bylaws may also need a public consultation meeting and approval by the MRC du Haut-Saint-Laurent. The public notice for each of these explains the steps, dates and who is eligible.

How a resolution is passed

A resolution is moved and seconded by council members, then voted on at the sitting. Each decision is recorded in the minutes (procès-verbal), which council approves at the next sitting and which are public. A resolution takes effect when adopted. No public notice is needed.

Under Ormstown's council procedure bylaw, 2-2026:

  • Council members must vote on every motion unless legally exempt because of a conflict of interest.
  • The mayor may suspend a decision by notifying the clerk in writing within 48 hours. Council can override that veto by re-adopting the resolution with an absolute majority of all its members.

The presiding member is generally not required to vote.

What this means for residents

  • Watch for notices of motion on council agendas. They tell you a bylaw is coming.
  • Read the draft. Copies must be made available to the public once it is tabled. Ask the municipality's clerk (greffe) if you can't find it.
  • Ask before the vote. Every regular sitting has two question periods. Written questions go to greffe@ormstown.ca by noon the Friday before the sitting (Bylaw 2-2026).
  • Compare the final version. At adoption, council must say what changed since the draft.
  • Check the public notice for the date a bylaw takes effect, and for any register or referendum you may be entitled to sign.
  • Read the minutes for the exact wording of each resolution.
