Ormstown’s string of suspicious fires: what is known so far

A pizzeria on Lambton Street was destroyed early Sunday, the fourth suspicious fire in Ormstown since July. The SQ has sent the Town a plan that adds officers downtown day and night, and council holds a special sitting on the fires Wednesday at 5 p.m. Police have not said whether the fires are connected.

The Ormstown Observer — November 9, 2026

A pizzeria on rue Lambton was destroyed by fire in the early hours of Sunday, Nov. 8. It is the fourth suspicious fire in Ormstown since late July, and it came four days after a Molotov cocktail was thrown at the same business. The Sûreté du Québec (SQ) is investigating the latest fire as a suspected arson and has not said whether the fires are connected (CBC).

Firefighters were called to Pizzeria Ormstown, formerly Pizza Sara, at about 2:30 a.m. SQ spokesperson Louis-Philippe Ruel said officers found several items at the scene suggesting the fire was set deliberately (CBC). He said the building was a total loss; no injuries were reported (CP24/CTV). The building also had apartments upstairs, and fire investigators and specialists were sent to the scene. As of Monday no suspect had been identified (Canadian Press, via Noovo). Some outlets call the business Sara Pizza.

Monday: the mayor meets the minister

On Monday morning Mayor Philippe Besombes met Quebec’s domestic security minister, Guy Lapointe Jr., and senior SQ officials. Officials committed to giving the mayor a concrete plan to prevent new incidents and strengthen the investigation; no date or details have been published (Canadian Press, via Noovo). Lapointe said the SQ will present the Town with a plan to reassure residents (Noovo). Radio-Canada reports he told reporters he is concerned and promised to address the number of officers available (Radio-Canada).

Radio-Canada reports the mayor said police resources would be added in the downtown area, day and night, starting Monday; the SQ has not published details. Besombes told the Canadian Press he left the meeting encouraged by officials’ willingness to act but not yet reassured about what comes next. Radio-Canada reports he said he was satisfied with the meeting.

By the mayor’s count, four SQ officers in two patrol cars cover the 13 municipalities of the Haut-Saint-Laurent overnight, which he said may be enough in normal times but not now. He also raised the fires’ economic impact with the province and the need to protect local businesses (Canadian Press, via Noovo). A store owner on the street told Radio-Canada that other businesses, not only restaurants, could be targeted, and residents the station spoke to suggested municipal surveillance cameras. Radio-Canada reports that all the attacks took place within about 100 metres of each other on Lambton Street.

The SQ’s plan

The minister had the SQ draw up an action plan, and the Town received it on Monday afternoon. Additional officers will be assigned to downtown Ormstown: during the day they will help with the investigation and meet business owners and residents, and at night they will patrol the downtown area (The Gleaner, Nov. 10). The mayor said the plan meets his requests and that he will stay in close contact with the SQ to make sure it stays in place as long as necessary (The Gleaner). The plan itself has not been published, and neither the SQ nor the Town has said how many officers it involves.

Besides the mayor and the minister, Monday’s meeting included MNA Frédéric Laplante, MRC du Haut-Saint-Laurent prefect Yves Métras, municipal councillors and SQ representatives. The mayor told The Gleaner that the tenants who lived above the pizzeria have been relocated, and that he expects to meet Ormstown business owners next week (The Gleaner).

Wednesday: a special council sitting on the fires

Council will hold a special sitting on Wednesday, Nov. 11, at 5 p.m. at Town Hall. The mayor called it, and the notice was published on Nov. 9. Its main item is a resolution on the recent fires, public safety and requests for help from the Québec government (notice of the special sitting, in French). The Gleaner reports that the resolution will set out the Town’s requests to Québec and that the mayor will ask neighbouring municipalities to support it (The Gleaner).

The agenda includes a 30-minute public question period. The text of the resolution was not published with the notice, and the notice does not mention a livestream. The Observer will report on what council adopts.

The four fires so far

  • July 20 · Casa Venezia, rue Lambton

    The restaurant was destroyed by fire (The Gleaner, Oct. 7). It has since moved to a neighbouring municipality, where the mayor says another Molotov cocktail was thrown at its new location (Radio-Canada).

  • Sept. 19 · Express 57, rue Lambton

    A fire that was deliberately set damaged the restaurant’s exterior (The Gleaner; CTV and CP24 date it Sept. 20).

  • Nov. 4, about 1 a.m. · Pizzeria Ormstown

    A Molotov cocktail was thrown at the front entrance. An SQ sergeant said the damage was minor and no one was hurt, and one person was arrested (The Gleaner, Nov. 5; CP24/CTV).

  • Nov. 8, about 2:30 a.m. · Pizzeria Ormstown

    The building was destroyed. The SQ is treating the fire as suspected arson (CBC). The tenants who lived above were relocated and no one was hurt, the mayor told The Gleaner.

The counts differ by outlet. CBC counts four suspicious fires in Ormstown, with the pizzeria hit twice. Mayor Besombes counts five fires in the area in a few months: four in Ormstown and one in the neighbouring municipality (Radio-Canada; CP24/CTV).

What police have said

The SQ is investigating all of the cases, and says it is too early to say whether they are related (CBC). By early October the SQ had increased its presence and night patrols on rue Lambton and in other commercial areas (The Gleaner, Oct. 7). Radio-Canada reports that Ormstown is one of 13 municipalities in the MRC du Haut-Saint-Laurent sharing four to five officers on night patrol, which the mayor considers too few (Radio-Canada).

What officials said before the meeting

Mayor Besombes called the situation unacceptable and said he had met the SQ three times in recent weeks. He believes the force needs more resources and asked the Québec government for help (CBC). He requested an urgent meeting with the minister, who said on X that residents have the right to feel safe and that he would be in Ormstown on Monday with the mayor, SQ representatives and MNA Frédéric Laplante (CBC; Radio-Canada). Claude DeBellefeuille, the federal member for Vallée-du-Haut-Saint-Laurent, said on Facebook that she agrees the situation is abnormal and unacceptable (CBC).

The mayor also plans to meet business owners about ways the Town can protect them. Two owners of businesses near the pizzeria told CBC they are frightened and are weighing whether to stay on the street (CBC).

How to help

A mother and her son lost their home and belongings in the Nov. 8 fire. An Ormstown resident has started a fundraiser to help them with clothing, food and other essentials (GoFundMe).

If you saw something or have video of any of the fires, contact the Sûreté du Québec first: 310-4141, or *4141 from a cellphone.

What is not known yet

  • Whether the four fires, the Nov. 4 arrest and the incident in the neighbouring municipality are connected. The SQ has not said so.
  • Who set the Nov. 8 fire. As of Monday no suspect had been identified (Canadian Press, via Noovo).
  • How many officers the SQ’s plan adds, and for how long. The plan itself has not been published.
  • What the resolution council votes on Wednesday will ask of Québec. Its text was not published with the notice.
