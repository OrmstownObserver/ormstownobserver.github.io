Ormstown’s string of suspicious fires: what is known so far

A pizzeria on Lambton Street was destroyed early Sunday, the fourth suspicious fire in Ormstown since July. Provincial police have not said whether they are connected.

The Ormstown Observer — November 9, 2026

A pizzeria on rue Lambton was destroyed by fire in the early hours of Sunday, Nov. 8. It is the fourth suspicious fire in Ormstown since late July, and it came four days after a Molotov cocktail was thrown at the same business. The Sûreté du Québec (SQ) is investigating the latest fire as a suspected arson and has not said whether the fires are connected (CBC).

Firefighters were called to Pizzeria Ormstown, formerly Pizza Sara, at about 2:30 a.m. SQ spokesperson Louis-Philippe Ruel said officers found several items at the scene suggesting the fire was set deliberately (CBC). He said the building was a total loss; no injuries were reported and no arrests have been made for this fire (CP24/CTV). Some outlets call the business Sara Pizza.

The four fires so far

  • July 20 · Casa Venezia, rue Lambton

    The restaurant was destroyed by fire (The Gleaner, Oct. 7). It has since moved to a neighbouring municipality, where the mayor says another Molotov cocktail was thrown at its new location (Radio-Canada).

  • Sept. 19 · Express 57, rue Lambton

    A fire that was deliberately set damaged the restaurant’s exterior (The Gleaner; CTV and CP24 date it Sept. 20).

  • Nov. 4, about 1 a.m. · Pizzeria Ormstown

    A Molotov cocktail was thrown at the front entrance. An SQ sergeant said the damage was minor and no one was hurt, and one person was arrested (The Gleaner, Nov. 5; CP24/CTV).

  • Nov. 8, about 2:30 a.m. · Pizzeria Ormstown

    The building was destroyed. The SQ is treating the fire as suspected arson (CBC).

The counts differ by outlet. CBC counts four suspicious fires in Ormstown, with the pizzeria hit twice. Mayor Philippe Besombes counts five fires in the area in a few months: four in Ormstown and one in the neighbouring municipality (Radio-Canada; CP24/CTV).

What police have said

The SQ is investigating all of the cases, and says it is too early to say whether they are related (CBC). By early October the SQ had increased its presence and night patrols on rue Lambton and in other commercial areas (The Gleaner, Oct. 7). Radio-Canada reports that Ormstown is one of 13 municipalities in the MRC du Haut-Saint-Laurent sharing four to five officers on night patrol, which the mayor considers too few (Radio-Canada).

What officials are saying

Mayor Besombes called the situation unacceptable and said he has met the SQ three times in recent weeks. He believes the force needs more resources and has asked the Québec government for help (CBC). He has requested an urgent meeting with Domestic Security Minister Guy Lapointe Jr., who said on X that he would be in Ormstown on Monday, Nov. 9, with the mayor, SQ representatives and MNA Frédéric Laplante (CBC; Radio-Canada). Claude DeBellefeuille, the federal member for Vallée-du-Haut-Saint-Laurent, said on Facebook that she agrees the situation is abnormal and unacceptable (CBC).

The mayor also plans to meet business owners about ways the Town can protect them. Two owners of businesses near the pizzeria told CBC they are frightened and are weighing whether to stay on the street (CBC).

What is not known yet

  • Whether the four fires, the Nov. 4 arrest and the incident in the neighbouring municipality are connected. The SQ has not said so.
  • Who set the Nov. 8 fire. As of Sunday afternoon, no arrests had been made (CP24/CTV).
  • What came out of Monday’s meeting with the minister. We found no published announcement of new police resources as of this writing.

This is the first story in the Observer’s community series on the fires. We will update it as new information is confirmed. Last updated Nov. 9, 2026.
