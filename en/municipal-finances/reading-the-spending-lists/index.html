What Ormstown’s spending lists show, and the budget update the mayor says is coming

At the November 2 sitting, Mayor Philippe Besombes said the expense lists attached to council minutes are raw data, not a budget report, and that keeping residents informed of budget follow-ups is a council objective for 2027. Here is what he said, what the lists contain, and where the budget comparison really lives.

The Ormstown Observer · The Ledger · November 5, 2026

Key takeaway

  • The list of payments attached to each set of minutes (“Annexe A”) mixes operating spending with work paid for by grants and loan bylaws. Adding it up does not tell you whether the budget was exceeded.
  • The mayor said the administration is working on a budget update that puts the figures in the right place, and that keeping residents informed of budget follow-ups is one of council’s objectives for 2027.
  • No format, date or frequency has been announced yet.
  • The documents that do compare spending with the budget are the two comparative statements, tabled on November 2 ahead of the 2027 budget sitting.

Why this came up

Every set of minutes Ormstown publishes ends with an annexe listing the payments council approved at that sitting: supplier, description and amount. The Observer’s Ledger is built from those annexes. The mayor said residents had been asking him whether the totals in those lists meant the Town was over budget. He closed his opening address on November 2 by answering that question (00:08:09).

What the mayor said

He described the figures in the annexe as raw data (00:08:09). In his account:

  • The lists include all of the Town’s spending, not only what is charged to the operating budget (00:08:09).
  • The periods covered vary. The start and end dates of the expenses in a list are not always the same (00:08:09).
  • Capital work is in there too. His example was this year’s road work: that spending appears in the same lists, so adding them up gives a total above the operating budget. Part of it, he said, is paid for by grants and loan bylaws (00:08:54).
  • Asked whether the Town is over budget, he said no: the two kinds of spending are simply mixed in one document (00:08:54).

Later in the sitting, when council approved the November 2 list of payments, he added that this list covers expenses only, while the annexe attached to the minutes also includes salaries and is more complete (00:14:26).

The change he announced

The mayor said council is working with the administration on a budget update for residents that puts the figures in the right place, so that people can follow how the budget is tracking. He called keeping residents informed of budget follow-ups one of council’s objectives for 2027 (00:08:54, 00:09:40).

That is the whole of the announcement. He did not say what the updates will contain, how often they will appear, or whether the annexe itself will change.

What the annexe contains today

The latest annexe on ormstown.ca is attached to the minutes of the September 8, 2026 sitting, where resolution 26-09-236 approves “la liste des opérations financières” in Annexe A.

  • It is organized by supplier, with a description and an invoice total. It does not show which budget line a payment is charged to, or whether it is funded by the operating budget, a grant or a loan bylaw.
  • Payroll is a single line covering salaries, source deductions and RRSP contributions, so no one’s pay can be read from it.
  • Some descriptions cite the resolution that authorized the spending (for example “Réso #26-06-163”), which lets you trace a payment back to a council decision.
  • Credits appear as negative amounts in brackets.

What this means for the Ledger

The Ledger carries the same caution on its method page: the monthly lists are council-approved outflows that include capital items financed by loan bylaws or grants, while the budget is kept on an accrual basis, so comparing the two gives an order of magnitude, never a share of “budget used.”

The Ledger cannot split a payment by funding source either, because the annexe does not say. If the Town’s budget follow-ups do, the Ledger will use them.

Where the budget comparison really lives

Under item 4.3 on November 2, council took note of the two comparative statements (2025–2026) (agenda; 00:16:03). The Municipal Code (art. 176.4) requires them every year:

  • The first compares revenue and spending so far this year, up to the end of the last month that ended at least 15 days before the tabling, with the same period last year.
  • The second compares the revenue and spending expected for the whole year with what the adopted budget planned.

They must be tabled at the last regular sitting held at least four weeks before the budget sitting. Tabled on November 2, that puts the 2027 budget sitting no earlier than November 30. The mayor said it is coming in a few weeks, at a special sitting where he will also review council’s first year (00:02:50). As of November 5 the statements were not posted on ormstown.ca.

What we don’t know yet

  • What the budget update and the 2027 follow-ups will contain, and how often they will be published.
  • Whether they will separate operating spending from work funded by grants and loan bylaws, payment by payment.
  • Whether the annexe attached to the minutes will change format.
  • The date of the 2027 budget sitting.
  • What the two comparative statements tabled on November 2 show.

What you can do

  • Read the lists with this in mind. A large monthly total can be road or water work paid by a grant or a loan bylaw, not overspending.
  • Ask for the comparative statements. If they are not posted with the minutes, the clerk’s office (greffe@ormstown.ca) can provide them.
  • Watch for the budget sitting notice. The Code requires a public notice at least eight days before. At that sitting, questions must be about the budget and how its spending is financed (art. 956).
  • Use the “Réso #” references in the annexe to find the decision behind a payment.
